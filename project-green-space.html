Urban Green Space Analysis

Spatial equity analysis of park access across census tracts — identifying underserved communities through network-based accessibility modelling.

Overview

This project examines how equitably green space is distributed across urban census tracts. Using pedestrian network buffers and demographic data from the US Census, I identified which neighbourhoods lack adequate park access and explored correlations with income and race.

Methodology

Park polygons were sourced from OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API. Census tract boundaries and demographic data came from the 2020 ACS 5-Year estimates. A 400 m and 800 m pedestrian network buffer was generated for each park using OSMNX in Python, then intersected with census tract geometries to calculate population within walking distance.

  • Fetched OSM park polygons using osmnx.geometries_from_place()
  • Computed network buffers at 400 m and 800 m walk distances
  • Joined ACS demographic tables to tract geometries via FIPS code
  • Calculated park access score: % of tract population within 800 m of a park
  • Visualised results with Matplotlib and exported final map in QGIS

Key Findings

Tracts in the southern and eastern districts had the lowest park-access scores (< 20% of residents within 800 m), and these areas were also significantly lower in median household income. The correlation between income quartile and green-space access was 0.61 (Pearson's r), suggesting a strong equity gap.
